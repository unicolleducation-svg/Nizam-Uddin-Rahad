PROFESSIONAL PHILOSOPHY

03 — A person-centred approach to healthcare

Rahad’s professional background brings together formal medical assistant education, supervised clinical placements, hospital employment and community welfare coordination. These complementary experiences provide a perspective on healthcare that extends beyond clinical procedures to the everyday needs of patients and their families.

Patient-centred support involves respecting dignity, privacy and individual preferences while helping people maintain comfort and independence. In a healthcare assistant role, careful observation, accurate communication and timely escalation of concerns are particularly important. His training and hospital experience provide a foundation for contributing to these activities within the responsibilities assigned by supervising professionals.

Professional direction

His international career provides a basis for adapting to different healthcare environments and working with people from diverse backgrounds. In Portugal, his community welfare experience complements his clinical background, while continued Portuguese language development supports integration into local professional settings.

CARE DELIVERY

05 — From observation to everyday patient support

Hospital-based experience in Bangladesh

At Hope Private Hospital, Rahad’s responsibilities included recording patient observations such as temperature, pulse, respiration and weight. These routine observations support the wider clinical team by providing information that can inform ongoing assessment. His work also included assistance with feeding, personal hygiene, mobility, bed-making and patient comfort.

These activities illustrate the importance of continuity in daily care: maintaining a clean environment, supporting patients with practical needs and communicating observations to the appropriate professionals. Healthcare assistants contribute to the quality of the patient experience through consistent attention to these fundamental tasks.

International healthcare experience in the United Kingdom

His employment with Victory Locum Limited adds UK healthcare experience to his international career.

09 — Clinical placements and transferable knowledge

Medicine and inpatient care

The medicine placement introduced supervised patient history-taking, observation of vital signs and inpatient follow-up. These activities helped connect classroom knowledge with the practical requirements of clinical documentation and patient monitoring.

Surgery and maternal health

Exposure to general surgery included theatre preparation, wound dressing and postoperative observation under supervision.
